# Annotated Bibliography

Key sources for the dossier, deduplicated across research domains and grouped by theme. **[P]** = primary source (original data/observation/description) · **[S]** = secondary (review, compilation, synthesis, or reportage). **Verification** refers to this project's independent citation-check pass (see `source-quality-audit.md`): *verified* = existence, authorship, year, venue, and topical content independently confirmed against a scholarly index; *unverified online* = standard bibliographic record consistent with the literature but not confirmable through this project's network environment. DOIs marked ✱ were checked for format and consistency but could not be dereferenced live (network policy).

## Live observation and imaging

- **Kubodera, T. & Mori, K. (2005).** First-ever observations of a live giant squid in the wild. *Proc. R. Soc. B* 272(1581):2583–2586. doi:10.1098/rspb.2005.3158✱ — **[P, verified]** The foundational live-observation paper: 2004 stills at 900 m, the 5.5-m severed tentacle, the "active predator" reframing. Nearly every later behavioral statement descends from it.
- **Robinson, N.J., Johnsen, S., Brooks, A., Frey, L., Judkins, H., Vecchione, M. & Widder, E.A. (2021).** Studying the swift, smart, and shy: unobtrusive camera-platforms for observing large deep-sea squid. *Deep-Sea Research I* 172:103538. doi:10.1016/j.dsr.2021.103538✱ — **[P, verified]** The only peer-reviewed account of a deep live observation since 2005: the Medusa/e-jelly method, the 2019 Gulf of Mexico encounter at 759 m, stalking behavior, encounters spanning 557–950 m.
- **Kubodera, T., et al. (2018; online 2016).** Extraordinary numbers of giant squid, *Architeuthis dux*, encountered in Japanese coastal waters of the Sea of Japan from January 2014 to March 2015. *Marine Biodiversity* 48:1391–1400. doi:10.1007/s12526-016-0618-7✱ — **[P, verified]** The 57-specimen influx: depth categories, size structure (all >160 cm ML female), and the "natural trap" hypothesis. Note: index gives Kubodera as first author; the full co-author list could not be itemized and is left as "et al."
- **Guerra, Á., González, Á.F. & Pierce, G.J. (2018).** First record on stranding of a live giant squid *Architeuthis dux* outside Japanese waters. *Ecology* 99:755–757. doi:10.1002/ecy.2073✱ — **[P, verified]** The 2016 Bares (Galicia) live stranding, plus the 60-record northern-Spain stranding series.
- **González, Á.F., et al. (2002).** Recent findings of the giant squid *Architeuthis* in northern Spanish waters. *J. Mar. Biol. Assoc. UK* 82. — **[P, verified]** Live fishery captures with real weights (148 kg/180 cm ML) — rare hard data for living females.
- **NOAA Ocean Exploration (2019).** "Journey into Midnight" expedition pages and mission log "Here Be Monsters" (Widder, Johnsen, Robinson). — **[P, unverified online]** Agency documentation of the 19 June 2019 first-in-US-waters sighting; corroborated by Robinson et al. 2021.
- **Press corpus for the 2006 and 2012 milestones** (AFP/Kyodo wire reports; *Japan Times* feature; National Geographic 2019 retrospective; Widder's Slate/NPR accounts). — **[S, unverified online]** The *only* documentation of the 2006 surface video and the 2012 depths/dates/priority — a structural weakness of the record itself, flagged wherever used. (Verification note: the Slate essay's attribution to Widder as author may be wrong; treated as reportage, not first-person testimony.)

## Specimens, size, and taxonomy

- **Steenstrup, J. (1857).** Oplysninger om Atlanterhavets colossale Blæksprutter. *Forhandl. Skand. Naturf.* 7:182–185. — **[P, unverified online]** The naming publication; corroborated by universal citation. The NHMD name-bearing material carries a "type status in doubt" flag.
- **Verrill, A.E. (1874).** The giant cuttle-fishes of Newfoundland… *The American Naturalist* 8. — **[P, verified]** Contemporaneous documentation that the 1873 dory-encounter arm was preserved at St. John's — the specimen core inside the Harvey legend.
- **Verrill, A.E. (1879–1881).** The gigantic squids (Architeuthis) and their allies. *Trans. Connecticut Acad.* 5:177–257. — **[P, verified]** First comprehensive scientific treatment of the Newfoundland series; also the source of the 17.37-m literature maximum later rejected as not first-hand.
- **Kirk, T.W. (1888).** Brief description of a new species of large decapod (*Architeuthis longimanus*). *Trans. NZ Inst.* 20. — **[P, unverified online]** The Lyall Bay "55 ft 2 in" (paced) specimen — the type case of the stretching confound.
- **Robson, G.C. (1933).** On *Architeuthis clarkei*… *Proc. Zool. Soc. London* 1933:681–697. — **[P, verified]** Exemplar of single-stranding species inflation; a British Isles record.
- **Förch, E.C. (1998).** The marine fauna of New Zealand: Architeuthidae. *NIWA Biodiversity Memoir* 110. — **[P, unverified online]** The NZ monograph; morphological case for one species.
- **Roeleveld, M.A.C. & Lipinski, M.R. (1991).** The giant squid *Architeuthis* in southern African waters. *J. Zool.* 224:431–477. doi:10.1111/j.1469-7998.1991.tb06036.x✱ — **[P, verified]** Southern African specimens; reviews the 18 nominal species; no basis for multiple species.
- **Sweeney, M.J. & Roper, C.F.E. (2001).** Records of *Architeuthis* specimens from published reports. Smithsonian NMNH. — **[S, unverified online]** The standard pre-2000 global record list; its own record count could not be confirmed — a documented loose end.
- **Guerra, Á., González, Á.F., Pascual, S. & Dawe, E.G. (2011).** The giant squid *Architeuthis*: an emblematic invertebrate… *Biological Conservation* 144:1989–1998. — **[P/S, verified]** The authoritative global specimen count (677) with regional tallies; conservation framing; canyon association.
- **McClain, C.R., et al. (2015).** Sizing ocean giants. *PeerJ* 3:e715. doi:10.7717/peerj.715✱ — **[P, verified]** Fixes 12 m as the longest scientifically verified total length; documents that "inaccurate reports of maximum sizes run rampant."
- **Paxton, C.G.M. (2016).** Unleashing the Kraken: on the maximum length in giant squid. *J. Zool.* 300:82–88. doi:10.1111/jzo.12347✱ — **[P, verified]** The disputed statistical case for 20-m plausibility and the 2.79-m "longest reliably measured" mantle length. Read together with the informal specialist rejections (press/blogs only — no formal rebuttal located).
- **O'Shea, S. & Bolstad, K.S. (2008).** Giant squid and colossal squid fact sheet. TONMO.com; and **ALCES Lab (2025)** update. — **[S, unverified online]** The most-cited expert size assessment (13 m/275 kg female, from ~130 specimens) and its 2025 upward revision (~2.75 m ML, 300 kg) — widely cited gray literature, flagged as such.
- **Romanov, E.V., et al. (2017).** A giant squid off Réunion Island… *J. Mar. Biol. Assoc. UK*. — **[P, verified]** Quantifies beak-allometry error in practice: one beak, mantle-length estimates 2,153–3,060 mm.
- **Ablett, J. (2012).** The giant squid… the making of an iconic specimen. *NatSCA News* 23:16–20. — **[P, verified]** Curatorial account of NHM London's 8.62-m "Archie" (Falklands, 2004).
- **Winkelmann, I., et al. & Gilbert, M.T.P. (2013).** Mitochondrial genome diversity and population structure of the giant squid *Architeuthis*. *Proc. R. Soc. B* 280:20130273. doi:10.1098/rspb.2013.0273✱ — **[P, verified]** The single-global-species result; 43 mitogenomes; expansion-vs-sweep ambiguity stated by the authors.
- **da Fonseca, R.R., et al. (2020).** A draft genome sequence of the elusive giant squid. *GigaScience* 9:giz152. doi:10.1093/gigascience/giz152✱ — **[P, verified]** The only nuclear genome resource (~2.7 Gb); no population analysis.
- **Roper, C.F.E. & Shea, E.K. (2013).** Unanswered questions about the giant squid *Architeuthis*. *Am. Malacol. Bull.* 31:109–122. doi:10.4003/006.031.0104✱ — **[S, verified]** The authoritative gap analysis as of 2013; source of the "300–1,000 m" restatement and the one-Mediterranean-record correction.
- **Roper, C.F.E., et al. (2015).** A compilation of recent records… western North Atlantic. *Am. Malacol. Bull.* 33:78–88. — **[S, verified]** Regional compilation, Newfoundland to Gulf of Mexico.
- **Salvador, R.B. & Tomotani, B.M. (2014).** The Kraken: when myth encounters science. *Hist. Ciênc. Saúde-Manguinhos* 21(3). — **[S, verified]** Peer-reviewed history clarifying what material underlies Steenstrup's names — and itself an example of myth persistence (repeats "up to 18 m").

## Distribution and habitat

- **Aldrich, F.A. (1968).** The distribution of giant squids… about the shores of Newfoundland. *Sarsia* 34:393–398; and **(1991)** *Bull. Mar. Sci.* 49:457–481. — **[P, verified]** The Newfoundland record and its ~30-year stranding periodicity.
- **Brix, O. (1983).** Giant squids may die when exposed to warm water currents. *Nature* 303:422–423. doi:10.1038/303422a0✱ — **[P, verified]** The only physiology experiment on the species (blood from the 1982 Radøy live capture): >4-fold O₂-affinity loss, 6.4→15 °C — the root of every thermal-limit argument.
- **Roper, C.F.E. & Boss, K.J. (1982).** The giant squid. *Scientific American* 246(4):96–105. — **[S, unverified online]** Traceable origin of the ubiquitous "300–1,000 m" depth statement.
- **Landman, N.H., et al. (2004).** Habitat and age of the giant squid inferred from isotopic analyses. *Marine Biology* 144:685–691. doi:10.1007/s00227-003-1245-y✱ — **[P, verified]** Isotope temperatures 10.5–12.9 °C (average depths 125–250 m) and the bomb-¹⁴C age constraint (≤14 y) — the sharpest counterpoints to deep encounters and short lifespans respectively.
- **Clarke, M.R., Denton, E.J. & Gilpin-Brown, J.B. (1979).** On the use of ammonium for buoyancy in squids. *JMBA UK* 59:259–276. — **[P, verified]** Quantitative anchor for ammoniacal buoyancy — why dead giant squid float.
- **Seibel, B.A., Thuesen, E.V. & Childress, J.J. (2000).** Light-limitation on predator-prey interactions… *Biol. Bull.* 198:284–298. — **[P, verified]** The only metabolic (enzyme) measurements on *Architeuthis* tissue.
- **Guerra, Á., et al. (2004).** A review of records… and severe injuries in *Architeuthis dux* stranded after acoustic exploration. ICES CM 2004/CC:29. — **[P, verified]** Original report of the Asturias 2001/2003 stranding-cluster/seismic-survey coincidence; conference paper, not journal peer review.
- **André, M., et al. (2011).** Low-frequency sounds induce acoustic trauma in cephalopods. *Front. Ecol. Environ.* 9:489–493; **Solé, M., et al. (2017).** *Sci. Rep.* 7:45899. — **[P; 2011 unverified online/2017 verified]** The mechanism experiments (on other cephalopod species) behind the seismic-stranding hypothesis.
- **Coro, G., et al. (2015).** Improving data quality to build a robust distribution model for *Architeuthis dux*. *Ecological Modelling* 305:29–39. — **[P, verified]** The only dedicated global habitat model — and a demonstration of how the depth assumption and floater-biased presences propagate.
- **Wada, T., et al. (2020).** Exploring a legendary giant squid: an environmental DNA approach. *Marine Biology* 167:160. doi:10.1007/s00227-020-03773-z✱ — **[P, verified]** First eDNA assay for the species; winter-positive/summer-negative in the Sea of Japan.
- **Nester, G., et al. (2026).** Environmental DNA reveals diverse and depth-stratified biodiversity in East Indian Ocean submarine canyons. *Environmental DNA*. doi:10.1002/edn3.70261✱ — **[P, verified]** eDNA range extension to Western Australia; authorship recovered during verification.
- **Cherel, Y. (2003).** New records of the giant squid *Architeuthis dux* in the southern Indian Ocean. *JMBA UK* 83:1295–1296. — **[P, verified]** Subantarctic beak records (sleeper sharks, seabird samples) to 47–48°S.
- **Roper, C.F.E. & Young, R.E. (1972).** First records of juvenile giant squid. *Proc. Biol. Soc. Wash.* 85:205–222. — **[P, verified]** First juveniles (from predator stomachs off Chile/Madeira) — and the reason the "juveniles near the surface" claim is weaker than commonly stated.

## Diet, predators, and the sperm whale interaction

- **Bolstad, K.S. & O'Shea, S. (2004).** Gut contents of a giant squid *Architeuthis dux* from New Zealand waters. *NZ J. Zool.* 31:15–21. — **[P, verified]** The NZ gut-content study: *Nototodarus* + conspecific tissue; cannibalism raised as possibility, autophagy weighed.
- **Deagle, B.E., et al. (2005).** Genetic screening for prey in the gut contents from a giant squid. *J. Hered.* 96. — **[P, verified]** First DNA diet screen: hoki identified; molecular cannibalism evidence. The correct source of the "hoki" claim often mis-attributed to Bolstad & O'Shea.
- **Lordan, C., Collins, M.A. & Perales-Raya, C. (1998).** Observations on morphology, age and diet of three *Architeuthis*… *JMBA UK* 78:903–917. — **[P, verified]** Irish males: stomach contents, morphology, and the sub-annual statolith ages.
- **Regueira, M., Guerra, Á., et al. (2014).** What does the giant squid *Architeuthis dux* eat? *Hydrobiologia* 725:49–55. — **[P, verified]** The synthetic diet analysis; trophic level 4.7; "opportunistic ambushing hunter" — one pole of the hunting-mode debate.
- **Pérez-Gándaras, G. & Guerra, Á. (1978).** Nueva cita de *Architeuthis*… descripción y alimentación. *Investigación Pesquera* 42(2):401–414. — **[P, verified; venue supplied from later citations]** Among the earliest gut-content reports.
- **Guerra, Á., et al. (2010).** Life-history traits of the giant squid revealed from stable isotope signatures recorded in beaks. *ICES J. Mar. Sci.* 67:1425–1431. doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsq091✱ — **[P, verified]** Ontogenetic trophic climb (~5.8‰ δ¹⁵N) and δ¹³C-stability evidence for restricted adult ranges.
- **Cherel, Y. & Hobson, K.A. (2005).** Stable isotopes, beaks and predators… *Proc. R. Soc. B* 272:1601–1607; **Cherel, Y., et al. (2009).** *Biol. Lett.* 5. — **[P, verified]** The beak-isotope method and the placement of *A. dux* within the deep-sea cephalopod assemblage.
- **Nilsson, D.-E., Warrant, E.J., Johnsen, S., Hanlon, R. & Shashar, N. (2012).** A unique advantage for giant eyes in giant squid. *Curr. Biol.* 22:683–688; **Schmitz, L., et al. (2013).** *BMC Evol. Biol.* 13:45; **Nilsson, D.-E., et al. (2013).** *BMC Evol. Biol.* 13:187. — **[P, all verified]** The eye-evolution debate, in full: hypothesis, allometric critique, rebuttal.
- **Roeleveld, M.A.C. (2002).** Tentacle morphology of the giant squid *Architeuthis*… *Bull. Mar. Sci.* 71:725–737. — **[P, verified; author recovered during verification]** The prey-capture apparatus: toothed sucker rings, no hooks.
- **Cherel, Y. & Duhamel, G. (2004).** Antarctic jaws: cephalopod prey of sharks in Kerguelen waters. *Deep-Sea Res. I* 51:17–31. — **[P, verified]** Sleeper sharks consuming large *A. dux*; the predation-vs-scavenging caveat.
- **Beasley, I., et al. (2019).** Stomach contents of long-finned pilot whales… Tasmania. *PLOS ONE* 14:e0206747. — **[P, verified]** Pilot whales as documented consumers.
- **Imber, M.J. (1992).** Cephalopods eaten by wandering albatrosses… *J. R. Soc. NZ* 22:243–263; **Xavier, J.C., Croxall, J.P. & Reid, K. (2003).** *Marine Biology* 142:611–622. — **[P, verified]** Juvenile Architeuthidae in albatross diets; the scavenging fractions (~30% by number/~85% by mass of cephalopod diet) — re-attributed to Xavier et al. during verification.
- **Clarke, M.R., Martins, H.R. & Pascoe, P. (1993).** The diet of sperm whales off the Azores. *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 339:67–82. — **[P, verified]** The best-quantified regional predation dataset: Architeuthidae 12.1% by estimated mass; modal prey 400–450 g.
- **Clarke, M.R. & Pascoe, P. (1997).** …sperm whale stranded at Penzance. *JMBA UK* 77; **Clarke, M.R., et al. (1998).** …Hawaiian shores. *JMBA UK* 78; **Clarke, M.R. & MacLeod, N. (1982).** *Mem. Natl. Mus. Victoria* 43:25–42 (venue corrected during verification); **Gaskin, D.E. & Cawthorn, M.W. (1967).** *NZ J. Mar. Freshw. Res.* 1; **Santos, M.B., et al. (1999).** *MEPS* 183; **(2002)** *JMBA UK* 82; **Foskolos, I., et al. (2020).** *Deep-Sea Res. I*. — **[P, all verified]** The regional diet mosaic: outlier whales, number-vs-mass asymmetry, and regions with no *Architeuthis* at all.
- **Clarke, M.R. (1980).** Cephalopoda in the diet of sperm whales of the southern hemisphere. *Discovery Reports* 37:1–324. — **[P, unverified online]** The foundational monograph; characterized here via **Cherel, Y. (2021)** (*Deep-Sea Res. I*; **[S, verified]**), whose taxonomic revision shows *Architeuthis* nowhere numerically dominant in southern whale diets.
- **Clarke, R. (1955).** A giant squid swallowed by a sperm whale. *Norsk Hvalfangst-Tidende* 44(10). — **[P, unverified online]** The classic intact whale-stomach specimen; commonly quoted measurements deliberately not repeated here pending sight of the original.
- **Clarke, M.R. (1996).** Cephalopods as prey. III. Cetaceans. *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 351; **Santos, M.B., Clarke, M.R. & Pierce, G.J. (2001).** *Fisheries Research* 52. — **[P/S, verified]** The consumption estimates ("may be over twice the biomass of fish caught by man") and the formal verdict that their confidence limits are "discouragingly wide."
- **Miller, P.J.O., et al. (2004).** *Proc. R. Soc. B* 271; **Watwood, S.L., et al. (2006).** *J. Anim. Ecol.* 75; **Teloni, V., et al. (2008).** *J. Exp. Mar. Biol. Ecol.* 354; **Madsen, P.T., et al. (2007).** *Aquatic Biology* 1; **Wilson, M., et al. (2007).** *Biol. Lett.* 3; **Mooney, T.A., et al. (2010).** *J. Exp. Biol.* 213; **Fais, A., et al. (2016).** *Sci. Rep.* 6:22205ff. — **[P, all verified]** The tag-and-acoustics corpus: whale dive/buzz statistics, squid as sonar targets, squid hearing limits, and the double rejection of acoustic stunning.
- **Roe, H.S.J. (1969).** The food and feeding habits of sperm whales taken off the west coast of Iceland. *J. Cons.* (ICES) 33. — **[P, verified]** The peer-reviewed anchor for sucker scars, including "abnormally large" ones.

## Reproduction, growth, and development

- **Norman, M.D. & Lu, C.C. (1997).** Sex in giant squid. *Nature* 389:683–684. doi:10.1038/39497✱ — **[P, verified]** First mated female; origin of the sperm-injection hypothesis.
- **Hoving, H.J.T., Roeleveld, M.A.C., Lipinski, M.R. & Melo, Y. (2004).** Reproductive system of the giant squid *Architeuthis* in South African waters. *J. Zool.* 264:153–169. — **[P, verified]** The anatomical backbone: terminal organ, "probable hectocotylization," fecundity 3.5–6.2 million, intermittent-spawning inference, male self-implantation.
- **Hoving, H.J.T., et al. (2006).** A male giant squid… from the Fladen Ground. *Basteria* 70:29–32; **Hoving, H.J.T. & Laptikhovsky, V. (2007).** Getting under the skin. *Biol. Bull.* 212:177–179; **Arkhipkin, A.I. & Laptikhovsky, V.V. (2010).** *J. Molluscan Stud.* 76:299–300. — **[P, all verified]** Self-implanted males; autonomous spermatophore implantation (in *Onykia*); the comparative context for the *Architeuthis* transfer puzzle.
- **Murai, R., et al. & Hirohashi, N. (2021).** All the spermatangia on a female were implanted by single-pair copulation… *Deep-Sea Res. I* 175:103585. — **[P, verified]** The paternity analysis (one male, 66 spermatangia).
- **Sasai, S., Tanaka, Y. & Hirohashi, N. (2025).** Evidence for direct use of terminal organ for spermatophore transfer… *microPublication Biology*; **Hirohashi, N., et al. (2025).** Discovery of adipocyte-like accessory reproductive cells… *microPublication Biology*. — **[P, verified]** The newest living-animal and histological evidence on male reproduction.
- **Ringvold, H., et al. (2021).** In situ recordings of large gelatinous spheres… egg mass of *Illex coindetii*. *Sci. Rep.* 11. — **[P, verified]** The egg-sphere misattribution, closed by DNA.
- **Jackson, G.D., Lu, C.C. & Dunning, M. (1991).** Growth rings within the statolith microstructure of the giant squid *Architeuthis*. *The Veliger* 34:331–334. — **[P, verified]** Where age research began.
- **Perales-Raya, C., et al. (2020).** How old are giant squids? First approach to aging *Architeuthis* beaks. *Bull. Mar. Sci.* 96(2). doi:10.5343/bms.2019.0041✱ — **[P, verified]** Beak ages (411–674 d), ~1.97 mm ML/day growth, ~3-y extrapolation — and the explicit framing of the 1–14-year controversy.
- **Grist, E.P.M. & Jackson, G.D. (2007).** How long would it take to become a giant squid? *Rev. Fish Biol. Fish.* 17. — **[P, verified]** The modeling middle ground (~3–6 y; the gloss is interpretive — the abstract stresses the wide possible range).
- **O'Shea, S., et al. (2022).** Description of paralarval giant squid *Architeuthis dux*… *Marine Biodiversity* 52:47. doi:10.1007/s12526-022-01287-z✱ — **[P, verified]** The smallest known specimens; ontogenetic-descent discussion. Co-author list beyond O'Shea not independently confirmed.
- **Wada, T., et al. (2015).** First records of small-sized young giant squid… *Mar. Biodivers. Rec.* 8:e153. — **[P, verified]** The Japanese juveniles (140.8–332 mm) — juveniles, not paralarvae.

## Myth, history, and misidentification

- **Pierce, S.K., et al. (1995).** On the giant octopus (*Octopus giganteus*) and the Bermuda Blob. *Biol. Bull.* 189:219–230; **Pierce, S.K., et al. (2004).** …the Chilean Blob…: nothing but whales. *Biol. Bull.* 206. — **[P, verified]** The definitive globster resolutions (collagen 1995; sperm whale DNA 2004).
- **Paxton, C.G.M., Knatterud, E. & Hedley, S.L. (2005).** Cetaceans, sex and sea serpents. *Arch. Nat. Hist.* 32(1). — **[P, verified]** The modern reassignment of Egede's 1734 monster to a cetacean; **Magin, U. (2023)** (*J. Anomalistics*; **[S, verified]**) adds the mirage variant.
- **McCarthy, J., Sebo, E. & Firth, M. (2023).** Parallels for cetacean trap feeding… across two millennia. *Mar. Mamm. Sci.* 39:830–841. — **[P, verified]** The hafgufa-as-whale argument — strongest published challenge to "medieval kraken = giant squid."
- **Latva, O. (2023).** *The Giant Squid in Transatlantic Culture: The Monsterization of Molluscs.* — **[S, verified]** Academic history arguing the continuous horror-kraken narrative is itself a construction.
- **Dyer, J. (2020).** Big stories about big squids: the *Britannia*… *War in History*. — **[P, verified]** The peer-reviewed debunking of the 1941 squid-attack legend.
- **France, R.L. (2016).** Reinterpreting nineteenth-century accounts of whales battling "sea serpents"… *Int. J. Marit. Hist.* 28:686–714. — **[P, verified]** Representative of the modern re-analysis of "sea serpent" reports as entangled whales.
- **Ellis, R. (1998).** *The Search for the Giant Squid.* Lyons Press. — **[S, verified as publication]** The standard scholarly-popular synthesis (Montfort, *Alecton*, *Pearl*, Heuvelmans, *Stein*); this review's fallback where primary documents were unreachable — used with that status declared.
- **Heuvelmans, B. (1965/1968).** *In the Wake of the Sea-Serpents*; **(2003)** *The Kraken and the Colossal Octopus.* — **[S, verified as publications]** The source of mid-century "super-squid" inflation; his specific size claims were deliberately not quoted pending page-level verification.
- **Primary historical texts** — Aristotle (*Hist. Anim.* IV), Pliny (*Nat. Hist.* 9), *Konungs skuggsjá* (c. 1250), Egede (1741), Pontoppidan (1752/1755), Denys de Montfort (1801–02), Bouyer's *Alecton* report (Comptes Rendus 53, 1861), Lee (1883), Bullen (1898), Tennyson (1830), Melville (1851), Verne (1869–70). — **[P as texts; assessed via verified secondary scholarship]** Cultural context and first-claimed events; none is biological evidence for *Architeuthis*.
