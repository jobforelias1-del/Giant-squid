# Observation Timeline — Specimens, Photographs, Recordings, Expeditions, and Live Observations

Chronological register of major events in the human record of *Architeuthis dux*.
**Status key:** ✅ **well-verified** (anchored to peer-reviewed, museum, or agency documentation confirmed in this review) · 🔶 **first-claimed** (event widely asserted; primary documentation weak, lost, or unlocated) · ⚠️ **disputed** (event real but a central detail — identity, measurement, cause, or priority — is contested).
Pre-scientific "sightings" are included only where they materially shaped the record; their assessment lives in `myths-and-evidence.md`.

## Before science (to 1856)

| Date | Event | Status |
|---|---|---|
| c. 350 BCE | Aristotle notes a large squid kind (*teuthos*) of ~five ells — earliest written notice of unusually large squid; link to *Architeuthis* unprovable | ✅ (as text) |
| c. 1250 | *Konungs skuggsjá* describes the island-like **hafgufa**, root of the kraken tradition; 2023 scholarship reads it as whale trap-feeding, not squid | ⚠️ |
| 1639 | Carcass strands at Þingeyrarsandur (Thingore Sand), Iceland — oldest generally accepted giant squid stranding; no material preserved | 🔶 |
| 1673 | First recorded Irish specimen landed at Dingle, Co. Kerry | 🔶 |
| 1741 | Egede publishes the 1734 Greenland "most dreadful monster"; modern analysis concludes a cetacean, not a squid | ⚠️ |
| 1755 | Pontoppidan's *Natural History of Norway* codifies the mile-scale kraken (its back "an English mile and a half in circumference") | ✅ (as text) |
| 1802 | Denys de Montfort's "poulpe colossal" discredits giant-cephalopod claims for decades | ✅ (as history) |
| 1853 (Dec) | Large cephalopod strands at Raabjerg, Denmark; cut up for bait; the salvaged beak becomes the basis of *Architeuthus monachus* | ✅ |
| 1855-11-05 | Capt. Vilhelm Hygom collects the Atlantic specimen (~31°N 76°W) that anchors the name *A. dux* (NHMD 77320 — museum flags "type status in doubt") | ✅ |

## The founding era (1857–1900)

| Date | Event | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **1857** | **Steenstrup publishes the name *Architeuthus dux*** (*Forhandl. Skand. Naturf.* 7:182–185) — the kraken becomes a described taxon | ✅ |
| 1861-11-30 | French aviso ***Alecton*** encounters a live giant squid off Tenerife; capture attempt recovers only a posterior fragment (lost); report to the Académie des Sciences met with skepticism. **First credible claimed live encounter** | 🔶 (event well attested; details unverifiable) |
| c. 1870–1881 | Newfoundland stranding cluster begins — the material basis of Verrill's monographs and of the 1870s squid publicity wave | ✅ |
| 1873-10 | Conception Bay/Portugal Cove, Newfoundland: fishermen sever an arm from a live squid at the surface; **the arm is preserved at St. John's and material reaches Verrill** (contemporaneously documented, Verrill 1874) — the narrative embellishments are Harvey's | ✅ (relic) / ⚠️ (story) |
| 1873-11/12 | Logy Bay, Newfoundland: near-intact specimen netted; Rev. Moses Harvey's "sponge-bath" photographs — **commonly cited as the first photographs of a giant squid** (no rigorous priority audit exists) | 🔶 (priority) / ✅ (specimen) |
| 1874-07 | London *Times* prints the schooner *Pearl* "sunk by a squid" story — regarded by historians as newspaper fiction | ⚠️ |
| 1877 | Vélain describes *Mouchezis sanctipauli* from St. Paul Island — southern-hemisphere nominal species later folded into *A. dux* | ✅ |
| 1878-11-02 | **Thimble Tickle** report: ~55 ft (16.8 m) total claimed, via a second-hand account in a Harvey letter; nothing measured or preserved; long listed as the size record | ⚠️ |
| 1879–1881 | Verrill's monographs (*Trans. Connecticut Acad.* 5) give the Newfoundland series its scientific description; an 1880 review hails the replacement of "Kraken" legends with "veritable specimens" | ✅ |
| 1887 | Lyall Bay, NZ: "*A. longimanus*" — 55 ft 2 in **paced, not taped**, mantle only ~1.8 m; the type case of post-mortem tentacle stretching | ⚠️ |
| 1896-11 | St. Augustine, Florida: multi-ton carcass named *Octopus giganteus* by Verrill, retracted by him within months; shown to be vertebrate collagen (1995) and finally pinned to whale blubber (2004) | ✅ (resolution) |
| 1898 | Frank Bullen's *Cruise of the Cachalot* publishes the archetypal whale-vs-squid surface "battle" — anecdote, never corroborated | ⚠️ |

## Specimens and stomachs (1900–1999)

| Date | Event | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1933-01 | Scarborough, Yorkshire stranding described as *Architeuthis clarkei* (Robson 1933; NHMUK 1933.1.30.5); now a synonym | ✅ |
| 1941 | Troopship *Britannia* sunk; the "giant squid took a shipmate" survivor tale becomes a wartime urban legend (debunked, Dyer 2020) | ⚠️ |
| 1954 | Ranheim, Norway: early color photographs of a nearly intact specimen; first Gulf of Mexico specimen found the same year | ✅ |
| 1955 | Robert Clarke reports a large, essentially intact giant squid from an Azores sperm whale stomach (*Norsk Hvalfangst-Tidende*) — the classic whale-stomach specimen; commonly quoted measurements unverified against the original | 🔶 |
| 1963–64 | Cook Strait whaling-station study (151 whales): *Architeuthis* present in sperm whale diet but not dominant (Gaskin & Cawthorn 1967) | ✅ |
| 1968–1991 | Aldrich documents Newfoundland stranding periodicity (~every third decade) and the 1960s specimen series | ✅ |
| 1969 | Roe documents squid-attributed sucker scars on sperm whales off Iceland, some "abnormally large" | ✅ |
| 1972 | **First juveniles described** (45 & 57 mm, from fish stomachs off Chile and Madeira; Roper & Young) | ✅ |
| 1976 | USS *Stein* sonar-dome damage attributed to an unknown large squid — primary documentation thin; not evidence of *Architeuthis* | ⚠️ |
| 1979 | Ammonium buoyancy of oceanic squid quantified (Clarke, Denton & Gilpin-Brown) — the physiology behind floating carcasses | ✅ |
| 1980–1982 | Malcolm Clarke's Southern Hemisphere sperm whale diet monograph (*Discovery Reports* 37) and Tasman Sea study — foundational predation datasets | ✅ |
| 1982-08-23 | Live capture at Radøy, Norway; the animal's blood yields the only *Architeuthis* physiology experiment (Brix 1983: O₂-affinity collapse 6.4→15 °C) | ✅ |
| 1991 | First report of growth increments in *Architeuthis* statoliths (Jackson, Lu & Dunning) — age research begins | ✅ |
| 1993 | Azores sperm whale diet quantified: Architeuthidae 12.1% of cephalopod mass (Clarke, Martins & Pascoe) | ✅ |
| 1995 | Three mature males trawled west of Ireland — morphology, diet, and sub-annual statolith ages (Lordan et al. 1998); St. Augustine tissue shown to be vertebrate collagen (Pierce et al.) | ✅ |
| 1997-10 | **First mated female** reported (*Nature*): spermatophores embedded in the skin of both ventral arms (Norman & Lu) | ✅ |
| 1998 | Förch's NIWA monograph on New Zealand Architeuthidae | ✅ |

## Seeing it alive (2000–present)

| Date | Event | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 2000–2001 | O'Shea's NZ expeditions capture the **first live paralarvae** (plankton tows); all die — the only known live-holding attempt | 🔶 (press/institutional record; specimens later formally described 2022) |
| 2001-09 & 2003-10 | Asturias stranding clusters coincide with airgun seismic surveys; acoustic-trauma cause claimed (Guerra et al.) — causation still disputed | 🔶/⚠️ |
| 2002-01-15 | Goshiki beach, Kyoto: claimed first photographs of a live adult at the surface — circulates only in secondary compilations; no primary record located | 🔶 |
| 2003-01 | Kersauson trimaran crew reports a squid on the hull off Madeira — no photo, no trace | 🔶 |
| 2004-03-15 | "Archie" (8.62 m female) trawled alive off the Falklands; preserved whole at NHM London | ✅ |
| **2004-09-30** | **First photographs of a live giant squid in its habitat**: baited rig at 900 m, Ogasawara; >550 stills; 5.5-m severed tentacle recovered (Kubodera & Mori 2005) | ✅ |
| 2004 | Landman et al.: bomb-radiocarbon and isotope study of Tasmanian statoliths — the 1-vs-14-year age conflict is born; Hoving et al. describe the reproductive system; Cherel & Duhamel report *A. dux* in Kerguelen sleeper shark stomachs; Bolstad & O'Shea report conspecific tissue in a gut | ✅ |
| 2005 | Deagle et al.: first DNA-based diet screen (hoki; molecular cannibalism evidence); Smithsonian acquires its Spanish female | ✅ |
| 2006-12-04 | **First video of a live adult** — a ~3.5-m female hooked at ~650 m off Ogasawara, filmed dying at the surface; **never published in a peer-reviewed venue** | ✅ (event, via press) |
| 2012-06/07 | **First video at depth** — NHK/Discovery expedition off Ogasawara: Widder's Medusa + e-jelly platform (~700 m) records footage days before Kubodera's Triton submersible dive (encounter ~630 m, followed to ~900 m; reported 10 July). Depths/dates/priority press-sourced; broadcast by NHK 13 Jan 2013 and Discovery 27 Jan 2013 | ✅ (event) / 🔶 (details) |
| 2012 | Nilsson et al. publish the giant-eye/sperm-whale-detection hypothesis (eye ~27 cm) — rebutted by Schmitz et al. 2013; debate open | ⚠️ (hypothesis) |
| 2013 | Winkelmann et al.: 43 mitogenomes → one global species; three young juveniles caught in Japanese coastal waters (described 2015) | ✅ |
| 2014-01 → 2015-03 | **Sea of Japan influx**: 57 specimens, 28 alive at discovery — the largest live-encounter cluster ever (Kubodera et al. 2018) | ✅ |
| 2015-12-24 | Toyama Bay marina: diver Akinobu Kimura films a live ~3.7-m animal at close range — the best in-water footage of an active giant squid | ✅ (press-documented) |
| 2016-10-07 | Bares, Galicia: live stranding of a 105-kg female — first documented live stranding outside Japan (Guerra et al. 2018, *Ecology*) | ✅ |
| 2016 | Paxton's "Unleashing the Kraken" reopens the maximum-size dispute (20 m "eminently plausible") | ⚠️ |
| **2019-06-19** | **First live giant squid filmed in US waters** — Medusa/e-jelly at 759 m, northern Gulf of Mexico ("Journey into Midnight"; published Robinson et al. 2021) | ✅ |
| 2020 | Draft nuclear genome (da Fonseca et al.); first eDNA detections (Sea of Japan, winter-only; Wada et al.); first beak-based ages (Perales-Raya et al.) | ✅ |
| 2021 | Single-male paternity of all spermatangia on one female (Murai et al.); diver-filmed "giant squid egg spheres" genetically shown to be *Illex coindetii* (Ringvold et al.) | ✅ |
| 2022-04-20 | Live stranding at Ugu beach, Obama City, Fukui; fate unconfirmed | ✅ (press) |
| 2022-09 | Formal description of the smallest known specimens — five NZ paralarvae, 6.7–8.8 mm (O'Shea et al., *Marine Biodiversity*) | ✅ |
| 2023-01-06 | Divers film a weak ~2.5-m animal off Toyooka, Hyōgo; press "first scuba video" claims conflict with the 2015 Toyama dive | ⚠️ (priority claim) |
| 2025 | Moribund males observed with terminal organs extended through their own funnels — first living evidence bearing on the mating mechanism (Sasai et al., *microPublication Biology*; publication verified; n = 2 moribund animals, not observed mating) | ✅ |
| 2025-03-09 | *(Comparison — different species)* First confirmed live **colossal squid** (*Mesonychoteuthis hamiltoni*): juvenile filmed at 600 m, South Sandwich Islands (Schmidt Ocean Institute) | ✅ |
| 2025-12 | Social-media video of an apparently healthy giant squid feeding on a diamondback squid at the surface — if verified, the first natural-feeding footage; currently unvetted | 🔶 |
| 2026 | eDNA detections extend the documented range to Western Australian submarine canyons (Nester et al., *Environmental DNA*) — eDNA presence only, not a specimen record | 🔶 |

## Reading the pattern

Three regimes are visible. **1639–1857:** carcasses accumulate as wonders. **1857–2000:** carcasses accumulate as data — taxonomy, anatomy, diet-via-whales — while the living animal in its habitat remains unobserved (surface encounters and live captures — 1861, 1873, 1982 — stay anecdotal or terminal). **2004–present:** roughly two decades of direct observation totaling a few hours across a handful of events — every deep-water one mediated by bait or lures, with only the 2004 stills and the 2019 encounter carrying peer-reviewed publications (Kubodera & Mori 2005; Robinson et al. 2021), while the surface/coastal record was published separately (Guerra et al. 2018; Kubodera et al. 2018). The first *claimed* events (Alecton 1861; Kyoto 2002; Medusa's exact first 2012 hit; the 2025 feeding clip) consistently precede the first *well-verified* equivalents (2004 stills; 2019 publication) by years to decades — a recurring signature of how knowledge of this animal actually accretes.
